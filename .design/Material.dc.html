Aurora

Warm firelight against a cold sky. Everything sits on a midnight ground with slow-moving aurora behind it, and every surface is one sheet of glass floating above that light — never a flat panel, never a hairline box. The mono type, the exposed hardware chrome and the all-lowercase labels are gone.

The material — three depths

Depth is blur radius, not shadow spread. A chip barely disturbs what is behind it; a sheet takes the whole ground with it. Every tier carries the same two inset lines — a bright one on the top edge where light catches the rim, a dark one on the bottom where the glass is thick.

The rim is the whole trick

Glass reads as glass because of what happens at its edge. One inset highlight along the top, one inset shade along the bottom, and a soft radial lens sitting off the top-left corner — a hint of the light source the aurora already implies. Drop those three and the same blur reads as a grey box.

Motion

Two speeds only. Things that respond to you move in about half a second and settle without a bounce. Things that live in the background — the aurora, a hero card breathing on its own — move over a minute, slow enough that you never catch them starting.

The curve
cubic-bezier(0.16, 0.84, 0.28, 1)

Nearly all of the distance is covered in the first third, then it coasts. Fast to start feels responsive; no overshoot keeps glass feeling heavy rather than springy.
